There is a particular kind of gap that opens when a technology arrives faster than a language does. In 2020, large language models were being demonstrated, debated and monetised in English — while a hundred and twenty million Ethiopians were reading about them in translation, or not at all. Jason Peters decided the translation should not come second-hand.

First a broadcaster

መጀመሪያ ተናጋሪ

He built ETHIO TECH with JayP — ኢትዮ ቴክ ከጄይፕ ጋር — from the ground up: research, scripting, editing and production all handled in-house, in Amharic, for an audience that was not being served. It grew to 84,700 subscribers and a reputation as the country's number one technology channel.

The ETV interview on ChatGPT was the moment it broke past the tech audience. Suddenly the conversation about generative AI was happening in living rooms, in Amharic, and it was being led by somebody who had actually used the tools rather than read a press release about them.

That route — practitioner first, explainer second — is why the channel has held credibility where a thousand generic "AI news" accounts have not. He is not forecasting from a distance. He is reporting from inside the build.

Then a founder

ከዚያ መስራች

Explaining a technology eventually stops being enough. ETN Ecosystem and the ET Apps Community are his attempt to move from commentary to infrastructure — decentralised platforms built on the TON blockchain, with an explicitly African frame of reference rather than an imported one.

The distinction matters. A great deal of Web3 in Africa has been built for foreign capital looking for a market. ETN reads as the inverse: a community of builders, in Ethiopia, working out what decentralised platforms are actually for when the local constraints are payment rails, connectivity and trust rather than speculative yield.

In 2025 he took that argument to Ethiopian Blockchain Week as an invited panelist — Africa's Web3 future, argued from an African standpoint.
